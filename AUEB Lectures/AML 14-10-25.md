## Generic Notation:

$$Y = f(X) + \epsilon$$
$f: \text{ systemcatic information X provides about Y}$
$\epsilon : \text{ is \textbf{unobserved random} variable, independent from X with 0 mean and variance Var(ε). Captures errors, unmeasured vars}$
We don't expect $Y$ to be precisely $f(X)$ when $f$ is given **Because** of $\epsilon$

With $f$ I can **Predict** $Y$ when $X$ takes value of $x$. When $X = x$ the prediction of $Y = f(x)$

$f(x)$ is:
- Generally $f$ is unknown and should be estimated from training dataset results.
- Statistical learning = set of approaches for estimating $f$
